# Support escalation

Version 0.1 · Prepared 2026-10-13 · Proposed operating practice pending owner adoption

Escalate to the owner immediately when discovered: suspected security/privacy incident; payment integrity problem; data loss; widespread outage; unsafe action requested; legal/insurance notice; unknown production authority; repeated failure of restoration; or likely breach of a client commitment.

For ordinary tickets escalate when the next promised update is at risk, scope is disputed, the assignee is unavailable, a vendor is blocking progress, or a workaround creates material risk. Do not wait for an arbitrary ticket-age threshold when impact is serious.

1. Summarize client/service, impact, timeline, evidence, actions tried, exact decision needed and deadline.
2. Owner names incident lead/backup and decides approved technical path and communication.
3. Contact a vendor only through verified support with minimal data and appropriate disclosure approval; record case ID and next check.
4. Involve counsel, broker/insurer, accountant or security specialist when their scope is triggered. Check policy notice/consent requirements before incurring response costs.
5. If no responder is available, use the documented continuity contact and client-approved contingency; record the gap and reduce future commitments until resolved.

No employee may spend money, accept legal terms, admit liability, issue refunds, waive fees, promise service credits, alter legal terms or expand access merely because a ticket is escalated.
