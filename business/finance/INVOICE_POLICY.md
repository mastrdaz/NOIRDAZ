# Invoice procedure

Version 0.1 · Prepared 2026-10-13 · Proposed operating practice pending owner adoption

Owner approves invoice terms from the executed agreement. Proposed default billing cadence is monthly for recurring service, but due days, deposit amount and service start must come from the signed SOW, never this document.

1. Verify legal seller/client identity, billing address/contact, contract/SOW/change-order reference and purchase order if required.
2. Match each charge to delivered milestone, agreed deposit or active service period. Show setup, recurring service, approved expenses, discounts/credits and taxes separately.
3. Use a unique sequential invoice ID, issue date, service dates, due date, currency, amount and approved payment route. Never include secret banking/card credentials in this public template.
4. Confirm taxability/sourcing/exemption evidence with approved bookkeeping rules; no universal “software is tax-free” label.
5. Owner checks amount, duplicate billing, paid deposit application and recipient. Send only with sending authorization.
6. Save the issued copy privately; record amount, due date, status and delivery evidence. Correct through a credit/reissue workflow with an audit trail, not silent edits.

## Recurring billing review

Before each billing run, reconcile active clients, contract start/end dates, approved price changes, usage, cancellations and credits. Confirm valid recurring-charge authority in the payment system. A new recurring amount, service or processor mandate needs appropriate approval. Compare successful charges, failed payments and invoices to the processor/bank settlement; schedule alone is not proof of collection.

## Deposits

Record contract-required deposit, receipt, application and remaining balance. Do not call a deposit nonrefundable unless the reviewed agreement and applicable law support that treatment. Bookkeeper decides liability/revenue treatment. Cleared funds are required only where the contract says so; record any owner-approved credit exception.
