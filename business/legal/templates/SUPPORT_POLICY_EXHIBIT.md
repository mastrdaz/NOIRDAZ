## 1 Covered services and access

Covered systems, environments, supported versions and tasks are [[SUPPORT_SCOPE]]. Excluded tasks are [[SUPPORT_EXCLUSIONS]]. Included support allowance, measurement unit, billing period and any rollover are [[SUPPORT_ALLOWANCE]]. Warranty correction under the MSA does not consume a paid support allowance unless an express negotiated provision states otherwise.

Authorized requesters are [[AUTHORIZED_SUPPORT_CONTACTS]]. Routine requests must use [[SUPPORT_INTAKE_CHANNEL]]. Security concerns use [[SECURITY_REPORTING_CHANNEL]]. Emergency escalation, if purchased, uses [[EMERGENCY_SUPPORT_CHANNEL]]. Provider will verify identity before granting access, resetting privileged access or disclosing confidential information. A person reporting an issue does not automatically have authority to approve expenses or scope changes.

## 2 Coverage and clocks

Staffed support hours, timezone and excluded holidays are [[SUPPORT_COVERAGE_HOURS]]. After-hours coverage is [[AFTER_HOURS_COVERAGE]]. If no after-hours service is purchased, the exhibit must say so explicitly. Receipt by an automated mailbox or alert system outside coverage does not represent a human response.

The response targets are [[RESPONSE_TARGET_SCHEDULE]]. That schedule must identify the priority, time target, whether measured in elapsed or covered business time, and the start and stop rules. An initial response is a qualified human acknowledgment and triage step; it is not a guaranteed restoration or resolution. Binding restoration or availability commitments exist only in the signed SLA [[SLA_EXHIBIT_REFERENCE]].

The clock starts when the request reaches the designated channel with reasonably sufficient identifying and impact information. Provider will promptly request missing material information. Any pause while awaiting essential Client action must be documented with the reason, request and restart time; a pause cannot conceal delay attributable to Provider.

## 3 Priority and escalation

Priority reflects actual impact and available workarounds:

- Critical: a production service is unavailable to substantially all intended users, or a credible material security incident requires immediate containment
- High: a major production function is materially impaired, affecting significant operations without a reasonable workaround
- Normal: a limited defect or degraded function with a reasonable workaround
- Request: advice, access administration, planned changes or enhancements

Provider will consider Client's reported impact, assign a priority and explain a material reclassification. The parties will not describe a feature request as an outage solely to accelerate the queue. A Security Incident still follows the MSA's incident-notice duties regardless of ticket priority.

Escalation contacts and timing are [[SUPPORT_ESCALATION_SCHEDULE]]. Material incident updates follow [[OUTAGE_COMMUNICATION_PROCESS]], including the channel, update cadence and owner. Updates will distinguish verified facts from estimates. Provider will not promise a restoration time it has not reasonably established.

## 4 Work authorization and changes

Provider may diagnose and perform the preauthorized routine support actions listed in [[PREAUTHORIZED_SUPPORT_ACTIONS]] within the purchased scope. Destructive actions, production data replacement, broad access changes, new third-party costs and work beyond an allowance require the designated approval unless a narrowly defined containment action is authorized under the MSA.

Rates and ceilings for approved additional work are [[APPROVED_RATE_SCHEDULE]] and [[SUPPORT_OVERAGE_AUTHORIZATION]]. Provider will notify Client before exhausting an allowance where reasonably foreseeable and obtain written approval before exceeding the authorized ceiling. Merely opening a ticket or receiving a diagnosis does not authorize a paid upgrade or unlimited overtime.

Planned changes follow the change-request process and agreed maintenance windows. Emergency containment will be limited to protecting affected systems, documented, and followed by a restoration plan. Provider will avoid altering unrelated Client services.

## 5 Client cooperation and vendors

Client will identify a reachable decision-maker, provide reproducible information, preserve relevant evidence and maintain the systems assigned to it. Secrets and sensitive records must use the secure channel rather than ticket text. Client will promptly report material configuration changes and third-party interventions affecting support.

Provider will coordinate vendor requests assigned to it but cannot control a vendor's resolution time. Provider will document the vendor case and next action. A third-party dependency does not excuse Provider's own agreed investigation, communication or escalation duties. If the problem falls outside scope, Provider will explain why and propose a separately approved next step.

## 6 Closure records and service changes

Provider may propose closure when the issue is resolved, an agreed workaround is in place, the request is completed, or Client confirms no further action is needed. Closure notes will identify the resolution or remaining limitation, checks performed and follow-up work. The inactivity process is [[TICKET_INACTIVITY_PROCESS]]. Administrative closure does not waive a warranty claim or establish project acceptance.

Support records are retained under [[SUPPORT_RECORD_RETENTION]], with access limited to legitimate service and legal needs. Any legal hold overrides routine deletion only for its defined scope. Reports supplied to Client will avoid other clients' data.

This exhibit lasts for [[SUPPORT_TERM]]. Fees and cancellation follow the SOW. Material changes to coverage, allowance, response targets or supported versions require the agreed written renewal or amendment; Provider may update internal routing without reducing the purchased service. End-of-support notice follows [[END_OF_SUPPORT_NOTICE]]. All remedies, liability limits and termination safeguards remain governed by the MSA and any expressly incorporated SLA.
