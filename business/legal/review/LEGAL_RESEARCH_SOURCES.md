# Legal research sources and review notes

DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW — ISSUE SPOTTING ONLY

Research date and retrieval date for the accessible sources below: 2026-10-13. Official primary sources and government guidance were used. They identify issues for review; they do not establish that the drafts are valid or that NOIRDAZ is compliant. This is a focused services-contract research pass, not a full multistate survey, case-law opinion or title/insurance investigation.

## Wisconsin business contract renewals

**Official reference:** Wisconsin State Law Library, Contract Law, page updated January 28, 2026. https://wilawlibrary.gov/topics/contract.php

The official library identifies Wis. Stat. § 134.49 for renewal and extension of business contracts. The Legislature's section page was linked by that source but could not be retrieved in this research session: https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/134/49

**Drafting implication:** Counsel must verify the current statute, applicability, exemptions, disclosure/initial/signature mechanics, reminder window and authorized notice method before approving automatic renewal. No detailed statutory deadline is represented here as independently verified. The base MSA requires a deliberate signed provision before automatic renewal; the optional clause is not represented as a completed statutory form.

**Specific threshold checks for counsel:** Verify the treatment of automatic renewal/extension periods longer than one month under subsection (2); the separate reminder rule where both the initial and renewal terms exceed one year under subsection (3); and the reported 15-to-60-day reminder window measured before the customer's nonrenewal deadline, rather than before service expiration. Verify the exclusion for an arrangement permitting termination of an automatically renewed period with no more than one month's notice, as well as other exclusions. Confirm the actual statutory delivery methods under subsection (4); ordinary contract email language should not be assumed sufficient. These are targeted verification questions, not a current-law opinion based on a successfully retrieved Legislature text.

## Wisconsin breach notification

**Official source:** Wisconsin Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection, Wisconsin's Data Breach Notification Law. Page undated; retrieved 2026-10-13. https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/Publications/IDTheftDataBreach607.aspx

DATCP explains § 134.98, its personal-information triggers and exceptions. Its general notice description requires notice within a reasonable time, no more than 45 days after learning of an unauthorized acquisition, with qualifications and law-enforcement considerations. The corresponding Legislature page could not be retrieved: https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/134/98

**Drafting implication:** The MSA's provider-to-client incident notification is deliberately separate from an individual's statutory notice. The contract must support investigation and earlier overlapping duties; 45 days is not a recommended contractual reporting target. Counsel must check affected residents, data, roles and all applicable laws.

## Copyright ownership and assignment

**Official source:** U.S. Copyright Office, Title 17 Chapter 2, particularly §§ 201 and 204. Current official page retrieved 2026-10-13. https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html

The statute distinguishes authorship, ownership and transfer, and addresses signed written transfers of copyright. A commissioned project should not rely solely on the label “work made for hire.”

**Drafting implication:** The MSA uses a signed assignment of identified deliverables, preserves Background Materials and third-party licenses, and requires a rights chain from personnel. Counsel must review scope, timing, employee/contractor agreements, statutory limitations and any rights beyond copyright.

## PCI script criteria for embedded forms

**Official source:** PCI Security Standards Council, FAQ 1588, February 2025. https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1588/

The FAQ explains a script-attack eligibility criterion for SAQ A e-commerce pages embedding a processor form. It describes relevant technical protections or qualifying confirmation from the payment provider and distinguishes redirects for that particular criterion. Other eligibility requirements remain relevant; the compliance-accepting entity determines the appropriate validation path.

**Drafting implication:** Capture actual browser/card flow and allocate script controls and evidence. Do not claim that a hosted iframe, token or redirect alone establishes compliance or questionnaire eligibility.

## PCI external scans for outsourced checkout

**Official source:** PCI Security Standards Council, FAQ 1604, June 2026. https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1604/

The FAQ confirms SAQ A external ASV scan requirements can apply to merchant e-commerce webpages that redirect to a payment provider or embed its iframe, even with outsourced payment processing.

**Drafting implication:** Assign scan ownership, scope and evidence with the acquirer or other compliance-accepting entity. Distinguish security testing NOIRDAZ can perform from a PCI Approved Scanning Vendor's validation service. This source prevents the mistaken assumption that redirect checkout eliminates all merchant PCI duties.

## HIPAA cloud and subcontractor relationships

**Official source:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Guidance on HIPAA and Cloud Computing. Original guidance 2016; current official page retrieved 2026-10-13. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html

HHS explains that a cloud provider maintaining electronic protected health information may be a business associate even if it lacks the decryption key. The guidance addresses appropriate BAAs and the need to align security, return and service terms.

**Drafting implication:** A generic NDA or services agreement is not a BAA. A planned prohibition on PHI does not resolve duties if actual PHI is received. The questionnaire gates regulated work pending facts, required agreements and operating safeguards.

## Security promises and service providers

**Official source:** Federal Trade Commission, Start with Security A Guide for Business. Current page retrieved 2026-10-13. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business

The FTC advises businesses to minimize data, restrict access, secure development and require and verify appropriate vendor security measures. Its examples are guidance drawn from enforcement matters, not universal technical specifications for every company.

**Drafting implication:** Define actual safeguards, evidence, subprocessors and review rights; avoid unsupported certifications and unenforceable operational promises. Security exhibits must match the implemented architecture and staffing.

## Financial services customer information

**Official source:** Federal Trade Commission, FTC Safeguards Rule What Your Business Needs to Know, December 2024; retrieved 2026-10-13. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know

The guide discusses covered activities and written security programs, including service-provider selection, contractual safeguards and ongoing review. An additional official FAQ discussing vendor oversight was reviewed: Automobile Dealers and the FTC's Safeguards Rule Frequently Asked Questions, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/automobile-dealers-ftcs-safeguards-rule-frequently-asked-questions (retrieved 2026-10-13).

**Drafting implication:** Determine actual regulated-client roles and security flow-downs. Ordinary payment integration does not automatically establish that Provider is a regulated financial institution, and working for a regulated business may introduce duties beyond this template.

## Private business website accessibility

**Official source:** U.S. Department of Justice, Guidance on Web Accessibility and the ADA, March 18, 2022. https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

DOJ describes accessibility obligations for covered public accommodations and practical accessibility considerations. The guidance expressly warns that it does not reflect the later state/local-government rule.

**Drafting implication:** Use an agreed technical target, test scope and remediation process while obtaining advice about the actual customer's obligations. Do not promise legal compliance from an automated scan, overlay or unverified conformance statement.

## State and local government web deadlines

**Official source:** U.S. Department of Justice, State and Local Governments First Steps Toward Complying with the ADA Title II Web and Mobile Application Accessibility Rule. Page dated January 8, 2025 and updated to discuss the April 2026 interim final rule. https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-rule-first-steps/

**Official rule reference:** April 2026 interim final rule, https://www.ada.gov/assets/pdfs/2026-ifr.pdf

The current DOJ guidance states that the interim final rule extended the deadline for entities with populations of 50,000 or more to April 26, 2027, and for smaller entities and special districts to April 26, 2028. These categories use the rule's population calculation, not NOIRDAZ's employee count.

**Drafting implication:** Obtain a public-sector-specific review and current deadline check before government or school work. Do not apply these dates as a universal private-business deadline or assume all other accessibility obligations are postponed.

## Federal negative option rule status

**Official sources:** FTC Negative Option Rule page, including its March 11, 2026 proposed-rule activity: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule

FTC Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, March 2026: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p064202negativeoptionruleanprm.pdf

These current materials show active rulemaking after the vacatur of the 2024 amendments. They should not be read as making a new proposal an operative final rule.

**Drafting implication:** Counsel should verify current federal law, online subscription requirements and applicable state B2B/consumer laws when approving recurring-service terms. Do not use a 2024 click-to-cancel summary as proof of current requirements.
