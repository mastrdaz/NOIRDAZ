# Sales Call / Conversation Guide

This should sound conversational, not memorized.

## Opening

“First, I really appreciated the call I had with your staff. The whole auction process has honestly been fun, and while we were talking I noticed one small part of the workflow that seemed like it might be easier for both your staff and bidders.

I’m not calling because I think you need to replace your auction system or redesign everything. I was thinking specifically about the payment step.”

## Explain the idea

“For normal payments that don’t require special handling, you could potentially include a secure Pay Online or Express Payment link in the winner email. The bidder could confirm the invoice, answer anything you need them to answer - for example whether the purchase is tax exempt - and, if the transaction is eligible, continue to a secure hosted checkout.

Anything that still needs staff review could continue exactly the way it does now.”

## Lower the perceived risk

“My preference would be to keep your auction data where it already lives and let your payment processor handle the actual card information. The small system I would build would act as the bridge between those two things rather than trying to become your auction system or your payment processor.”

## Ask before prescribing Square

“What are you currently using when someone calls in with a credit card? If that system already has a good hosted checkout or API, I’d rather work with what you already have. If it doesn’t, Square is one option I can show you.”

## About Noirdaz / transparency

“I also want to be straightforward about who you’d be working with. I work full time in healthcare laboratory diagnostics. Computer systems and software have always been the other major part of what I do, and I’ve been building and operating several working systems and sites of my own.

I’m now turning that work into a small client-facing business called Noirdaz Industries. It is intentionally small. You would be dealing directly with me, and nothing would be changed in your production workflow without your approval.”

If specifically asked about commercial client history, answer directly and accurately.

## Offer the demo

“If you’re open to it, I’d rather show you something than sell you an idea over the phone. If you can answer a handful of questions about how invoices and payments work now, I can build a small working demo using fake/test data and show you exactly what the bidder and your staff would see.

Then I can give you a rollout plan, firm pricing, what support looks like, and what I would need from you. You can review all of it before deciding whether you want to move forward.”

## Questions to prioritize live

1. What generates the invoice/winner email?
2. What do you currently process phone cards through?
3. What cases actually require the phone call/manual review?
4. How is an invoice marked paid now?
5. Does your auction software expose API/export/import capability?
6. Roughly how many phone/email card payments are you handling?
7. Would a small pilot be preferable to a full rollout?

## Closing

“That’s really all I’m proposing at this point: let me understand the workflow and build a no-pressure demo. If it doesn’t make your process clearly easier, there’s no reason for you to change it.”

## Do not say

- “Your website is outdated.”
- “You need Square.”
- “I can definitely integrate with your auction database” before seeing it.
- “PCI compliant” as a blanket claim about Noirdaz.
- “We never touch any customer data” unless the final architecture truly supports that statement.
- “Unlimited support.”
- made-up client counts, uptime claims, security certifications, or experience.
